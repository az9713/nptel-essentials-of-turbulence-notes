  • Reynolds 1883, An experimental investigation of the circumstances which determine whether the motion of water shall be direct or sinuous (Phil. Trans. R. Soc. Lond. 174, 935-982)

    p. 941: 'fitted with trumpet mouthpieces, so that water might enter without disturbance'. p. 943: U_s = (1/B_s) P/D, B_s = 43.79; 'the critical velocity was very sensitive to disturbance in the water before entering the tubes'; 'the greatest care as to the uniformity of the temperature of the tank and the stillness of the water'; 'the steady motion was unstable for large disturbances long before the critical velocity was reached'; eddies appeared in a 'full-blown manner'. p. 946: a second critical velocity at which existing eddies die out, 'This conclusion has been verified.' p. 948: v_c = (1/278) P/D; 'the ratio being 43.7 to 278'; nature of the surface affects the law of resistance above the critical velocity.

  • Schubauer and Skramstad 1948, Laminar-boundary-layer oscillations and transition on a flat plate (NACA Report 909)

    p. 1: wave motion with the characteristics predicted by a stability theory based on exponential growth of small disturbances; contributions of Tollmien and Schlichting; stability depends on kinematic viscosity, boundary-layer thickness, outer speed and frequency or wavelength. p. 2: 'transition involves two things'; 'Sufficiently small disturbance cannot produce transition, but a small disturbance may grow according to stability theory until it is sufficiently large to cause the flow to become turbulent'; delta* = 1.72 (nu x/U0)^(1/2) for the Blasius distribution; R = U0 delta*/nu = 1.72 R_x^(1/2). p. 7, Figure 7: effect of turbulence 100 sqrt((u'^2+v'^2+w'^2)/3)/U0 on the x-Reynolds number of transition (read from the graph). p. 30: 'experimentally established lower limit R = 450'; transition delayed until R about 2000; 'If no disturbances were present, theoretically transition should not occur'; 'good agreement with theory'.

  • Meseguer and Trefethen 2003, Linearized pipe flow to Reynolds number 10^7 (J. Comput. Phys. 186, 178-197)

    p. 1: eigenvalues for R from 10^2 to 10^7. p. 4: R = a U_CL/nu. pp. 10-11: rightmost eigenvalues with negative real parts scaling as R^-1, R^-1/2, R^-1/3 for the modes studied. p. 13: eigenvalues of the operators in question all in the left half-plane. p. 15: 'Transition to turbulence, like turbulence itself, depends on nonlinearity'; L 'itself is eigenvalue stable'; plane Poiseuille eigenvalue instability at R ~ 5772; transition observed at R ~ 350 (plane Couette), 1000 (plane Poiseuille), 2000 (pipe, continued p. 16).

  • Darrigol 2002, Stability and instability in nineteenth-century fluid mechanics (Revue d'histoire des mathematiques 8, 5-65)

    p. 49: Rayleigh 1880 on two-dimensional parallel flow; vorticity equation (19) for inviscid 2D flow; linearization with u, v ~ exp(i(kx - sigma t)). p. 50: stability equation (21) (U - sigma/k)(v'' - k^2 v) - U'' v = 0; multiplication by v* and integration, eqs. (22)-(25); if U'' has constant sign, Im(sigma) = 0; Rayleigh noted the criterion is of no help for the jet, whose U'' changes sign.

  • Blasius 1908, The boundary layers in fluids with little friction (NACA TM 1256)

    TM p. 7: xi = (1/2) sqrt(rho u/k) y/sqrt(x), psi = sqrt(k u/rho) sqrt(x) zeta, u = (1/2) u zeta', zeta zeta'' = -zeta''', zeta(0) = zeta'(0) = 0, zeta'(inf) = 2 (k the viscosity).

  • Taylor 1922, Diffusion by continuous movements (Proc. London Math. Soc. (2) 20, 196-212; read 10 June 1920)

    p. 196: 'turbulent motion is capable of diffusing heat and other diffusible properties through the interior of a fluid in much the same way that molecular agitation gives rise to molecular diffusion'. p. 197: heat conveyed across unit area = -rho sigma beta [u(x-a)]; [u(x-a)] = (1/2) d[X^2]/dt; turbulence uniformly distributed. p. 206: R_xi defined as the correlation of a particle's velocity at an interval xi. p. 207: eq. (18) [X^2] = 2[u^2] int int R_xi; eq. (19) short times. p. 208: lim int R_xi = I finite; eq. (20) [X^2] ~ 2 I T [u^2] at large T.

  • U.S. Standard Atmosphere, 1976 (NOAA-S/T 76-1562, NASA-TM-X-74335)

    p. 18: gamma = 1.40 (ratio of specific heats). p. 19: eq. (51) mu = beta T^(3/2)/(T + S), beta = 1.458e-6, S = 110.4 K; eq. (53) k_t = 2.64638e-3 T^(3/2)/(T + 245.4 x 10^(-12/T)). p. 20, Table 10: P0 = 1.01325e5 N/m^2, T0 = 288.15 K, rho0 = 1.2250 kg/m^3, kinematic viscosity 1.4607e-5 m^2/s.

    Order-of-magnitude balance k ~ eps^2; two-sided flat-plate drag 1.327 b (k rho l U^3)^(1/2), b the plate width, k the viscosity.

  • McComb, Berera, Yoffe, Linkmann 2015, Energy transfer and dissipation in forced isotropic turbulence (Phys. Rev. E 91, 043013)

    p. 2: 'epsilon = C_eps U^3/L, where U is the root-mean-square velocity and L is the integral scale', given by Taylor in 1935 (their attribution); p. 1: C_eps,inf = 0.468 +/- 0.006 and C = 18.9 +/- 1.3 for R_L up to 5875; p. 3: Donzis et al. about 0.4, Bos et al. about 0.5; p. 7, eq. 32: epsilon = A3 U^3/L + A2 nu U^2/L^2. Citation Phys. Rev. E 91, 043013 (2015) confirmed on https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/52798/.

  • Yeung, Sreenivasan, Pope 2018, Effects of finite spatial and temporal resolution in DNS (Phys. Rev. Fluids 3, 064603)

    dx/eta ~ 2.96/(k_max eta); k_max eta ~ 1.5 adequate for low-order statistics; intermittency requires more; Courant number with beta ~ 12.

  • Pope 2004, Ten questions concerning the large-eddy simulation of turbulent flows (New J. Phys. 6, 35)

  • Boffetta and Ecke 2012, Two-dimensional turbulence (Annu. Rev. Fluid Mech. 44, 427-451)

  • Lorenz 1963, Deterministic nonperiodic flow (J. Atmos. Sci. 20, 130-141)

    p. 130 abstract: 'nonperiodic solutions are ordinarily unstable with respect to small modifications, so that slightly differing initial states can evolve into considerably different states'. p. 135: equations (25)-(27); X proportional to the intensity of convective motion, Y to the temperature difference between ascending and descending currents, Z to the distortion of the vertical temperature profile; sigma the Prandtl number, r = Ra/Rc; 'their solutions cannot be expected to resemble those of (17) and (18) when strong convection occurs, in view of the extreme truncation'; origin: all roots negative when r < 1; steady states X = Y = +/- sqrt(b(r-1)), Z = r - 1. p. 136: eq. (34) critical r; sigma = 10, b = 8/3, critical r = 470/19 = 24.74; r = 28; steady states (+/-6 sqrt 2, +/-6 sqrt 2, 27).   • Wilczek, Daitche, Friedrich 2011, On the velocity distribution in homogeneous isotropic turbulence (J. Fluid Mech. 676, 191-217; arXiv:1107.0139v1)

    p. 2: the central-limit argument for a Gaussian velocity PDF 'turns out to be rather hand-waving as the assumption of statistical independence is obviously violated'; vorticity PDF has extremely non-Gaussian tails. p. 14: 'although the PDF is similar to Gaussian, significant deviations occur. Especially notable is the sub-Gaussian tail, which has also been found in experiments and other numerical simulations'. p. 25: 'the velocity PDF displays slight deviations from a Gaussian distribution with sub-Gaussian tails'.

  • Kerr 1983, Higher-order derivative correlations and the alignment of small-scale structures in isotropic numerical turbulence (NASA TM 84407)

    p. 1 (abstract): velocity- and mixed-derivative skewnesses do not increase with Reynolds number; R_lambda to 82.9. p. 2: limited range of scales 'restricts the Reynolds number to very low values'. p. 15: 'The value for the velocity-derivative skewness for simulations F11 to F27 is -0.505 +/- 0.005.' p. 17: F1 equals the velocity-derivative flatness if isotropy is assumed. p. 35, Table 3: F1 = 3.39 at R_lambda = 9.0 and 5.11 at 82.9; S_u from -0.497 to -0.523 over the forced runs F1-F27.

  • Yeung, Sreenivasan, Pope 2018, Effects of finite spatial and temporal resolution in DNS (Phys. Rev. Fluids 3, 064603)

    p. 064603-6: 'longitudinal velocity gradients are negatively skewed but transverse gradients, while symmetric, are more strongly non-Gaussian'; improved resolution reduces 'the likelihood of large fluctuations up to several thousands times the mean value' of dissipation and enstrophy.
