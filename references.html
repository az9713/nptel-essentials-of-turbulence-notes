  • Kolmogorov 1941, The local structure of turbulence (English translation, Proc. R. Soc. Lond. A 434, 1991)

    Cascade of pulsations ending where viscosity prevents further refinement; definitions of the length, time and velocity scales; rescaling to unit viscosity and dissipation; eps = 15 nu a^2 under local isotropy.

  • Blasius 1908, The boundary layers in fluids with little friction (NACA TM 1256)

    Order-of-magnitude balance k ~ eps^2; two-sided flat-plate drag 1.327 b (k rho l U^3)^(1/2), b the plate width, k the viscosity.

  • McComb, Berera, Yoffe, Linkmann 2015, Energy transfer and dissipation in forced isotropic turbulence (Phys. Rev. E 91, 043013)

    p. 2: 'epsilon = C_eps U^3/L, where U is the root-mean-square velocity and L is the integral scale', given by Taylor in 1935 (their attribution); p. 1: C_eps,inf = 0.468 +/- 0.006 and C = 18.9 +/- 1.3 for R_L up to 5875; p. 3: Donzis et al. about 0.4, Bos et al. about 0.5; p. 7, eq. 32: epsilon = A3 U^3/L + A2 nu U^2/L^2. Citation Phys. Rev. E 91, 043013 (2015) confirmed on https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/52798/.

  • Yeung, Sreenivasan, Pope 2018, Effects of finite spatial and temporal resolution in DNS (Phys. Rev. Fluids 3, 064603)

    dx/eta ~ 2.96/(k_max eta); k_max eta ~ 1.5 adequate for low-order statistics; intermittency requires more; Courant number with beta ~ 12.

  • Pope 2004, Ten questions concerning the large-eddy simulation of turbulent flows (New J. Phys. 6, 35)

    p. 6: 'With the numerical methods usually employed, halving the grid spacing increases the computational cost by about a factor of 2^4 = 16.' p. 5: 'halving the grid spacing h typically increases the required memory and CPU time by factors of 8 and 16, respectively.' p. 4: computational requirements increase as a power of Reynolds number; near-wall structures scale with the viscous length; Bradshaw: 'in the viscous near-wall region there are no large eddies'.

  • AMS Glossary of Meteorology, Coriolis parameter

    f = 2 Omega sin(latitude); fV is the horizontal Coriolis force per unit mass.

  • AMS Glossary of Meteorology, angular velocity of the earth

    Omega = 7.2921e-5 rad/s.

  • Lecture 14 recording

    Jet velocity record and mean profile; continuum hypothesis; Eulerian and Lagrangian fields; material derivative; continuity; momentum and Newtonian stress; Module 03 source slide (Pope 2000).

  • Lecture 15 recording

    Navier-Stokes and Euler equations, boundary conditions; pressure Poisson equation; conserved passive scalar.

  • Lecture 16 recording

    Vorticity equation; rates of strain and rotation; transformation properties; Reynolds-number similarity; invariance.

  • U.S. Standard Atmosphere, 1976 (NOAA-S/T 76-1562; NASA-TM-X-74335)

    Section 1.3.8: 'The mean free path L is the mean value of the distances traveled by each of the neutral particles, in a selected volume, between successive collisions with other particles in that volume', eq. (47). Table 10, 'Sea-level values of atmospheric properties': C_s,0 = 3.40294e2 m/s; L_0 = 6.6328e-8 m; N_0 = 2.5470e25 m^-3; T_0 = 2.8815e2 K; eta_0 (kinematic viscosity) = 1.4607e-5 m^2/s; mu_0 = 1.7894e-5 kg/(m s); rho_0 = 1.2250 kg/m^3.

  • Hunt, Wray, Moin 1988, Eddies, streams, and convergence zones in turbulent flows (CTR Proceedings of the Summer Program 1988, pp. 193-208)

    Eq. (2.1b): 'II = (du_i/dx_j)(du_j/dx_i) = E_ij^2 - (1/2) omega_i^2', E_ij the symmetric strain tensor; criterion (a) II < -II_E for eddy zones ('the irrotational straining is small compared with the vorticity'); criterion (b) a pressure minimum in the zone, eq. (2.2).

  • Boffetta and Ecke 2012, Two-dimensional turbulence (Annu. Rev. Fluid Mech. 44, 427-451)

    p. 428: 2D turbulence 'is never realized in nature or in the laboratory'; 'large-scale motions in the atmosphere and oceans are described, to first approximation, as 2D turbulent fluids'; the review is devoted to 'stationary, forced-dissipated, 2D turbulence'. p. 430: eq. (6) dZ/dt = -2 nu P; 'Because Equation 6 bounds enstrophy from above, Equation 5 implies that eps_nu -> 0 as nu -> 0. This is the main difference with respect to 3D turbulence in which Z can be amplified by vortex stretching ... resulting in finite energy dissipation in the limit of vanishing viscosity'; energy 'transferred to large scales by the inverse cascade'.
